Across \NEligible{} eligible federal appellate opinions, \Prevalence{} contain at least one
of thirteen frozen expressions of non-resolution. Declining to decide is routine,
announced in a vocabulary that has held its shape for a century, and rising in
every era we can measure.

Supervised systems learn the distinction where it matters. On H1 the sparse model
reaches 0.947 against the dictionary's \LexHOne{}, on the court-held-out condition an
encoder reaches \EncCourt{} against \LexCourt{}, and the H1 learning curve crosses the
dictionary near \LCCross{} items and keeps climbing, so more annotation should buy more.
Context helps to $\pm256$ characters and degrades beyond it, for models and for a human annotator alike. The task is
therefore discourse classification rather than phrase recognition, and the
dictionary's pooled lead is a property of anchor-centred sampling.

For the empirical study of courts, the consequence is that a citation is a
coarser instrument than it is usually taken to be. Network accounts of precedent
read an edge as evidence of the cited opinion's authority
\citep{fowler2007network, black2013citation}, but an edge ascribing a holding to
an opinion that expressly declined to reach the question means something
different from one that follows a real holding, and the edge treats them alike.
In \NIMEsc{} of \NIMDistinct{} hand-verified propositions a later court described as settled what the
originating court had reserved. Ten cases make a case series rather than a rate,
and they show the failure is real and, with an instrument of this kind, countable.

Retrieval and summarization over case law inherit the same assumption, that a
citation's proposition was decided where it was cited. Separating disposition
from discussion is a prerequisite for legal information systems rather than an
application.
